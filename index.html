The Great Prompt-Off

A live prompt-engineering competition, scored field by field.

Participants write a strategy for pulling six findings out of synthetic knee MRI reports, try it against public reports, then submit once against a hidden set while the leaderboard runs on the projector. The hard part was trust: hidden reports and answer keys never leave the server, and the scorer accepts only exact controlled labels.

synthetic, non-PHI knee MRI reports
5
public test attempts per participant
45

    TowerLogic

    Screenshot in, decision out.

    An experimental Clash Royale agent that plays from pixels. It pulls frames from an Android emulator, reads the hand with a PyTorch classifier, finds units with an ONNX detector, folds everything into one normalised game state, and scores candidate placements with a learned policy. A research project, not a production bot, and not affiliated with Supercell.

    Follow one frame

    A 640×640 YOLO-style detector, run in ONNX Runtime, finds units and towers; a compact classifier reads the four cards in hand.

    The monitor runs a small simulation of an arena to show each stage.

    Piano MIR

    Does the recording match the score?

    A Rust-first music information retrieval system for known-score performance assessment. Given a score and a WAV recording, it maps the score onto the audio timeline, checks the evidence for every expected note and its chroma, and reports timing and tempo apart from pitch.

    Play the test piece

    Minuet in G, BWV Anh. 114: the project’s main test pair. This is a synthesized rendering of its MIDI, 207 notes, and the keyboard on the desk plays from the same note list.

    Mosaic

    Find it once. Keep it.

    A Pinterest-style discovery app built for the Change++ Fall 2026 coding challenge: search, save, organise into collections and sections, share with privacy controls, follow, comment and message, and lay a collection out freely on a draggable Canvas. The Express and SQLite backend runs in transactions with permission checks, rate limiting and security headers, covered by end-to-end and security regression tests.
